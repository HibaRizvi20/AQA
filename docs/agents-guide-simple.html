AQA · in plain language

The QA agents, explained simply

No jargon. What this thing is, who does what, and why you can trust it — using a small team of specialists as the picture in your head.

The big idea

Testing an app by hand is slow and easy to get wrong. AQA is like hiring a small, careful QA team that never sleeps — except each "team member" is a software agent with one job it's very good at. You hand them a task ("test the checkout page"), and they take it from a rough idea all the way to finished, checked tests.

The important part: a real person stays in charge. The team does the heavy lifting, but at five points along the way it stops and waits for a human to say "yes, keep going." It's an assistant team, not an autopilot.

Meet the team

Think of a job moving down a line of desks. Each desk has a specialist who does their bit and passes it on.

The reader

1 · Scope Analyst

Reads the request and works out what actually needs testing — and what's out of scope. If the request and the real app disagree, they raise a hand instead of guessing.

The planner

2 · Test Architect

Turns that into a plan: what to check, and how many "normal", "should-be-blocked", and "weird edge" situations to cover.

The writer

3 · Case Designer

Writes each test in plain sentences anyone can read ("When I pay, then I see the order confirmed") — and automatically labels each one by area and type so you can pick just the ones you want later.

The filer

4 · Tracker Publisher

Logs the agreed tests into your issue tracker — but shows you a preview first and sends nothing until you confirm.

The coder

5 · Test Generator

Turns those plain-English tests into real automated code. Crucially, it checks every button and field against the live app, so the tests match reality, not a stale document.

The fixer

6 · Triage / Self-Healer

When a test fails, it figures out why: a flaky test (fixes it), a bad test (fixes it), or a genuine app bug (writes it up with evidence). It never quietly makes a test pass to hide a problem.

The checker

7 · Reviewer

Goes down a checklist before anything ships — labels present, no shortcuts, everything verified — and leaves review notes. It comments; it doesn't merge on its own.

The librarian

8 · KB Curator

Keeps a tidy record of what the app does and what we test, so the knowledge doesn't live only in someone's head.

Four helpers working in the background

These four don't hold up the line — they run alongside and just report what they find.

  • Performance & Logs — watches how fast things run and flags anything too slow.
  • Drift Detector — notices when the app changes so tests don't silently go stale.
  • Design-Parity — compares the built app against the original design and flags differences.
  • Eval Analyst — if the app has an AI assistant, grades its answers against a set of known-good ones.

How a job flows

1
Understand & plan
the reader and planner figure out what to test.
✋ Checkpoint 1 — a person reviews the plan before any tests are written.
2
Write the tests in plain English
the writer drafts and labels each one.
✋ Checkpoint 2 — a person approves the tests.
3
Turn them into code
the coder builds them and checks every step against the live app.
✋ Checkpoint 3 — a person reviews the code.
4
Run them & sort out failures
the fixer sorts flaky vs. real bugs.
✋ Checkpoint 4 — a person decides what to do about failures.
5
Final check & hand off
the checker reviews; the librarian records it.
✋ Checkpoint 5 — a person gives the final approval to merge.

Can I just use one of them?

Yes. You don't have to run the whole line. Need only a plan? Use the planner. Just want to grade the AI assistant? Use the Eval helper. Small one-off tasks are packaged as "skills" you can call on their own. Rule of thumb: the whole team for a whole feature, one specialist for one step.

Why you can trust it

1. The living app is the truth. Tests are checked against the real running app, not a document that might be out of date.

2. Nothing goes out without a "yes". Anything that leaves the workshop — filing a ticket, opening a request — is shown to you first and only sent when you confirm.

3. It never fakes a pass. If something can't be checked, it says so plainly instead of pretending everything's fine.

That's the whole thing: a careful specialist team that moves fast, shows its work, and always leaves a human holding the final "go".
